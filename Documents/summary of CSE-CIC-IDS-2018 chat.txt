Here is the executive brief of our engineering sprint and the architectural plan moving forward:

The Goal:
Test your hybrid IDS (trained on the 2017 lab dataset) against the unseen 2018 AWS cloud dataset.

What We Did:

Preprocessed 5.2M Rows: We stripped out bad data, enforced strict 2017 feature alignment, and safely applied your frozen 2017 scaler.

Calibrated Autoencoders: We established new anomaly thresholds based purely on the new 2018 "Normal" traffic to prevent false alarms.

Ran a Benchmark & Autopsy: We pushed 100,000 flows through your cascade pipeline and used a diagnostic script to track exactly how each model behaved.

The Results (The Reality Check):

Accuracy: 49.39%.

The Problem (Covariate Shift): The CNN caused 33,000+ False Positives. Because it was heavily trained on 2017 lab noise, the new 2018 AWS background noise confused it, causing it to flag normal cloud traffic as attacks.

The Success: Your unsupervised Masked Autoencoder (MAE) performed brilliantly, adapting to the new environment with only ~800 false positives. Your hybrid theory is sound.

The Solution (What You Document):
You do not need to throw the pipeline away. You will document this exact failure to prove that static models cannot survive network migrations without adaptation.

To solve this, you will utilize your "Network Engineer Retraining" feature for Domain Adaptation:

The admin feeds the IDS a small sample of the new network's traffic.

The system fits a new scaler to understand the local noise.

The system freezes the CNN's feature-extraction layers (so it doesn't forget 2017 attacks) and only fine-tunes the final classification layers to learn the new "Normal."
